## 1. Wellness & biohacking devices: what is growing, and what's the data?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced growth is in at-home light-therapy/LED masks (Gen Z is shifting spend from skincare to devices, and Shark now claims #1 facial-device brand), smart rings (Q2 2026 shipments +85% YoY, but Oura holds ~85% of the market, so it's not dropship-friendly), massage/recovery devices (a top TikTok Shop tool category), and sleep accessories (mouth tape and sleep bonnets show search spikes). I found no hard 2026 data on sauna blankets, cold plunges, acupressure mats, posture correctors or weighted blankets. One seller-tool source puts sauna-blanket search growth at only ~8%, which suggests that fad is cooling.

**Mouth tape safety**
- A 2025 PLOS ONE systematic review found "uncertain" and "minimal" evidence that mouth taping treats mouth breathing, OSA or sleep-disordered breathing. Risks include suffocation with nasal obstruction, aspiration if vomiting, and masking of undiagnosed sleep apnea. — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0323643); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12094774/)

### Inferences
- **Best dropship fits:** mouth tape (ultra-light, cheap, consumable/repeat purchase), sleep bonnets/eye masks, massage guns and mini percussion massagers, posture correctors, acupressure mats, and LED masks. LED masks are higher AOV ($50–$400+), light, and video-demonstrable.
- **LED masks are the strongest "device" opportunity but carry the most compliance exposure.** Sellers should source only units with a verifiable K-number whose cleared indication matches the ad copy. "FDA approved" claims or uncleared masks marketed for wrinkles/acne invite takedowns. The device also competes with well-funded brands (Shark, CurrentBody, Omnilux, Solawave) at the top end.
- **Avoid as dropship products:** smart rings (Oura dominance, subscription ecosystem, high returns/sizing issues), cold plunges (bulky, heavy, electrical/chiller), and sauna blankets (bulky, heat/burn liability, weak search growth).
- Mouth tape should be marketed for comfort and habit, not as a snoring or sleep-apnea treatment. Disease claims would convert it into an unapproved medical-device claim and raise FTC risk.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified in session] Electronic wellness devices (LED masks, massage guns, sleep gadgets) sold into the EU** need:
  - CE marking under the Low Voltage, EMC and RoHS directives, plus the Radio Equipment Directive if Bluetooth/app-connected.
  - Compliance with the EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 for rechargeable units.
  - WEEE/EPR producer registration in each country of sale (e.g., Germany ElektroG/stiftung ear, France). German marketplaces check EPR numbers.
  - For GB, UKCA/CE plus the UK WEEE regime.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified] LED masks marketed with medical claims** (e.g., "treats acne") are medical devices under the EU MDR (Regulation (EU) 2017/745) and need notified-body CE certification. High-intensity light equipment for skin treatment without a medical purpose falls under MDR Annex XVI. Low-power cosmetic LED masks are generally outside Annex XVI, but claims and intensity determine the boundary. Net effect: a US 510(k) clearance does not carry over to the EU or UK.
- **Europe-specific demand:** no EU/UK sales data for these devices was obtained. McKinsey's Future of Wellness survey covers multiple countries, but the secondary coverage retrieved did not break out European results.

### Gaps
- **EU/UK:** no European sales or search-trend data for LED masks, massage guns, mouth tape or smart rings. No verified EU/UK regulatory text, because official hosts were blocked and the search budget was exhausted.
- No Circana/NielsenIQ unit or dollar data for LED masks, massage guns or sleep accessories in 2026 (circana.com not reachable; no public figures found).

## 2. GLP-1 companion demand: which accessory and companion products are rising?

### Takeaway
GLP-1 users are reshaping demand toward protein (about a third buy protein shakes/bars regularly), fiber, and muscle-preservation nutrition. They are also creating a visible niche in pen travel cases/coolers. Hair-loss and skin-firming adjacencies are heavily marketed but weakly substantiated, which makes claims there risky. Market-size estimates range from ~$3.6B to $225.9B depending on definition, so they are unusable for sizing.

### Cited Findings
- About one-third of GLP-1 users now regularly buy protein shakes, powders and bars (Acosta Group data). — [NutraIngredients, Oct 16 2026 (via search summary)](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/10/16/five-ways-glp-1-drugs-are-altering-consumer-demand-for-supplements/)
- Protein beverages/shakes are expected to hold the largest share of GLP-1 companion foods in 2026. — [ResearchAndMarkets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6288346/glp-1-companion-foods-market-size-share)
- A GLP-1 companion foods forecast reaches US$16.58B by 2036 at 16.5% CAGR, with muscle preservation the leading nutritional focus (press release Oct 28, 2026). — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/28/3390453/28124/en/global-glp-1-companion-foods-market-size-share-trends-and-forecast-2026-2036-industry-to-reach-us-16-58-billion-as-protein-rich-innovation-and-telehealth-expansion-drive-16-5-cagr.html)
- Size estimates conflict widely: ~US$5.2B for "companion products" in 2026. — [Fact.MR](https://www.factmr.com/report/glp-1-companion-products-market). Versus ~US$225.9B, likely a much broader definition. — [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/glp-1-companion-products-market-report)
- Tate & Lyle's 2026 Ingredient Tracker: 63% of consumers intend to increase fiber intake over the next year. Suppliers are pairing fiber with protein in shakes, bars and mini-meals. — [NutritionInsight](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/protein-fiber-taste-glp-1-companion-nutrition.html)
- Fiber/constipation support is a defined companion category. A vendor playbook says GLP-1-associated hair shedding peaks at 3–6 months of treatment. — [Peakfinity Labs (vendor)](https://peakfinitylabs.com/blog/launch-glp1-companion-supplement-brand)
- Evidence caution: the sources reviewed do not establish that collagen or biotin prevent GLP-1-associated hair shedding, so hair-loss claims would need substantiation. — [Intermountain Nutrition](https://intermountainnutrition.com/glp-1-companion-supplements/)
- Pen cases and travel coolers are a visible retail category: insulated cases for Zepbound/Mounjaro/Ozempic pens, some with temperature displays and multi-pen capacity. — [Amazon search results](https://www.amazon.com/glp-pen-case/s?k=glp+pen+case); [VialCase guide](https://www.vialcase.com/blogs/news/best-ozempic-wegovy-mounjaro-carry-cases-amazon); [Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/market/glp_1_case_for_fridge)
- Pen users spend ~$2/month on supplies (needles, swabs); vial users spend more because they need reconstitution items. — [Rx.com](https://rx.com/education/glp1-injection-supplies-cost-2026)
- Amazon's supplement policy reportedly lists GLP-1 receptor agonists (and peptides such as BPC-157, TB-500 and Ipamorelin) as prohibited unapproved-drug ingredients. — [EvolveAMZ](https://evolveamz.com/amazon-dietary-supplement-policy/); [AMZBase](https://amzbase.com/guides/sell-dietary-supplements-on-amazon/)

### Inferences
- **Best dropship fits:** GLP-1 pen travel cases and insulated coolers (light, non-perishable, low return rate, not brand-dominated, $15–$40 price point), pill/injection-day organizers, portion-control plates and bowls, protein shaker bottles, resistance bands and light home-strength gear for muscle preservation, and body-firming tools (gua sha, body rollers).
- **Avoid:** anything implying GLP-1 effects ("nature's Ozempic", "GLP-1 booster" supplements). These attract FTC/FDA scrutiny and Amazon prohibition. Avoid medical supplies (needles, sharps) and compounded-drug adjacency.
- Protein and fiber products have real demand, but they are ingestibles dominated by large CPG brands and carry supplement compliance burdens (see Section 4). They are a poor fit for generic dropshipping.
- Hair-loss companion products (scalp serums, microneedling rollers, LED caps) ride GLP-1 shedding demand. Claims must stay cosmetic ("fuller-looking hair"), since drug claims ("regrows hair", "stops hair loss") convert a cosmetic into an unapproved drug.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified in session]** Wegovy and Mounjaro are prescribed in the UK (including via online pharmacies) and in major EU markets, so pen-case and companion-accessory demand plausibly exists in Europe too. No European usage figures were obtained.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified]** EU/GB nutrition-and-health-claims rules (EU Reg. 1924/2006 and the GB register) permit only pre-authorized health claims. "GLP-1 support", "appetite control" or weight-loss claims on supplements or foods would generally be unlawful in the EU/UK. That makes non-ingestible accessories (pen cases, portion plates, resistance bands) the only clean GLP-1-adjacent dropship play in Europe.

### Gaps
- **EU/UK:** no data on GLP-1 user numbers or companion-product demand in Europe.
- No quantified data found on GLP-1-driven demand for smaller-size apparel, resistance bands, portion plates or skin-firming products. Searches returned only pen-case results.
- Acosta/NutraIngredients details (number of GLP-1 users, other categories affected) could not be read in full (site blocked).
- No GLP-1 pen-case sales volume data found.

Beauty is TikTok Shop's #1 US category (~$2.08B in H1 2026 per Circana). **Regulatory: MoCRA (US cosmetics)**
- Under MoCRA, the "responsible person" is the manufacturer, packer or distributor named on the label. These include gua sha and ice rollers, scalp massagers and scalp-care brushes (riding the hair-serum/scalp "skinification" wave), heatless curlers, LED/microcurrent devices (with clearance caveats), press-on nails (light, cheap, high repeat, fashion-driven, not brand-dominated), nail tools, makeup organizers and beauty fridges (moderate weight, but no data found), and men's grooming tools (trimmers, body groomers).
- **K-beauty skincare itself is a weak dropship fit.** Brand-led demand (Medicube, Anua) means authorized-reseller and counterfeit risk. Formulas are liquids/creams (leakage, expiry), MoCRA labeling applies, and Korea-origin goods face tariffs (Section 7). White-label "K-beauty style" ingredient products (PDRN, snail mucin, collagen) via US-based fulfillers are more viable.
- **Fragrance dupes:** the mass fragrance boom (+15%) supports demand. However, "dupe" marketing that names designer brands carries trademark/false-association risk, and liquids/alcohol-based fragrance are hazmat (flammable) for shipping. Treat as a high-risk dropship category.
- **Hair tools:** high TikTok demand, but the value tier is now owned by branded players (Shark, Laifen). Generic "Dyson dupes" face poor reviews, high returns and electrical-safety (UL/ETL) risk. Mid-risk, mid-reward.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified in session] EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) 1223/2009** requires every cosmetic placed on the EU market to have:
  - an EU-established Responsible Person;
  - a Cosmetic Product Safety Report signed by a qualified safety assessor and a Product Information File;
  - notification on the EU CPNP portal before sale;
  - local-language labelling.
  GB has a parallel regime: UK Responsible Person plus notification via the UK "Submit Cosmetic Product Notification" (SCPN) service. Northern Ireland follows EU rules. Shipping a China- or Korea-made cream straight to an EU/UK consumer with no EU/UK RP is non-compliant. This is a much higher bar than US MoCRA's small-business exemption, and makes formulated-cosmetic dropshipping into Europe largely unviable without an EU/UK RP-holding supplier.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified] Nail products:** the EU banned TPO (trimethylbenzoyl diphenylphosphine oxide), a photoinitiator common in gel/UV nail polish, as a CMR substance from 1 Sept 2025 (Omnibus act). Chinese gel-nail kits and some semi-cured "gel" press-ons may contain it. Standard adhesive press-ons are less affected, but suppliers should certify TPO-free for EU sales.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified] Actives limits:** EU restrictions adopted in 2024 cap retinol/retinyl esters (e.g., 0.3% RE face, 0.05% body), alpha-arbutin/arbutin and kojic acid, with phase-in deadlines in 2025–2027. Some US- or Korea-formulated "high-strength" serums may be non-compliant in the EU.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified] Beauty devices** (hair dryers, stylers, LED masks, microcurrent) need CE/UKCA electrical conformity plus WEEE/battery EPR registration, as in Section 1. Plug type (EU Type C/F vs. UK Type G) adds SKU complexity, and generic dupes with US plugs are a common EU/UK return driver.
- **Europe-specific demand:** none of the retrieved sources gave 2026 EU/UK beauty sales. TikTok Shop operates in the UK and several EU markets (Spain, Germany, France, Italy, Ireland), but no TikTok Shop UK/EU category data was obtained.

### Inferences
- **Supplements are high-demand but high-risk for dropshippers.** Seller-of-record liability for adulteration and claims, Amazon's TIC/cGMP gate, MoCRA-like expectations from TikTok Shop, perishability/expiry dates, lot traceability, and heat sensitivity (gummies melt; probiotics degrade) all cut against dropshipping. Fulfillment by a US-based private-label supplement manufacturer (cGMP-certified, with COAs) is the only defensible model.
- **Claims to avoid:** "lowers cortisol", "fixes adrenal fatigue", "GLP-1 / Ozempic alternative", "treats anxiety/ADHD", "cures bloating/IBS", "hormone balancing" for menopause. These are disease or drug-like claims that FDA/FTC actively cite. Structure/function claims need the DSHEA disclaimer and substantiation.
- **Lower-risk ingestible-adjacent items:** creatine and protein accessories (shaker bottles, scoop funnels, pill organizers, travel supplement cases), magnesium body-spray/lotion (topical cosmetic rather than supplement, though still MoCRA-labeled), and mushroom-coffee gear (frothers, mugs).
- Creatine for women and magnesium (sleep/stress) are the clearest durable demand signals. Colostrum's growth is from a small base and may be faddish.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified in session] EU supplement regime is stricter and fragmented:**
  1. The Food Supplements Directive 2002/46/EC harmonizes only vitamins and minerals. Botanicals and other substances are regulated nationally. Many member states (e.g., Germany, France, Italy, Belgium) require product notification before sale, and maximum doses differ by country.
  2. The Nutrition and Health Claims Regulation (EC) 1924/2006 allows only claims on the EU authorized register. Magnesium ("contributes to a reduction of tiredness and fatigue", "normal muscle function") and creatine (performance in short-term high-intensity exercise at ~3 g/day for adults) have authorized claims. "Lowers cortisol", "gut healing", "immune boost from colostrum" or "adaptogen" claims generally do not.
  3. The Novel Food Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 requires pre-authorization for ingredients not significantly consumed in the EU before 15 May 1997. CBD is treated as novel and unauthorized. The status of some mushroom extracts and shilajit should be checked in the EU Novel Food Status Catalogue before selling.
  4. Some countries have restricted ashwagandha (e.g., Denmark).
- **[EU/UK background, unverified]** GB has its own register of authorized claims and an FSA novel-food process, broadly mirroring EU rules post-Brexit.
- **Net inference:** supplement dropshipping into the EU/UK is even less viable than in the US, because of national notifications, local-language labels, claim restrictions and novel-food risk. Supplement accessories (shakers, pill organizers, frothers) are the low-risk European play.

### Gaps
- **EU/UK:** no 2026 European supplement sales data (e.g., NielsenIQ EU, Euromonitor) for creatine, magnesium or colostrum. EU novel-food status of shilajit, lion's mane and other mushroom extracts was not verified (food.ec.europa.eu blocked).
- No 2026 SPINS/NIQ data found for cortisol-support supplements, gut-health/probiotic growth, or women-specific creatine sales.
- No source directly addressed how FDA/FTC/Amazon treat dropshippers versus brand owners. The inference that the seller/label-named party bears liability needs legal confirmation.

## 7. Tariff exposure: which products are most exposed, and which are commonly made outside China?

### Takeaway
As of November 2026, de minimis is gone for China/HK (since May 2025) and suspended for all countries (since Aug 2025, made indefinite June 2026). Every parcel needs a formal entry and duties. After the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs (Feb 2026) and Section 122 surcharges lapsed (July 2026), a new Section 301 tariff (12.5% for China and ~60 economies) took effect July 24, 2026, on top of pre-existing China Section 301 duties. China-made pet products and electronic wellness/beauty devices carry the highest stacked rates. Korean cosmetics now face ~12.5–15% tariffs. Sources are mostly vendor blogs and conflict on specific rates. In Europe, the binding constraints are compliance rather than tariffs: GPSR responsible operator (sourced), cosmetics RP/CPNP, EPR, and the phase-out of EU/UK low-value import relief (unverified background).

### Cited Findings
- China/HK lost commercial de minimis on May 2, 2025. CBP made the global de minimis suspension indefinite in June 2026. The Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling did not restore de minimis, since that rests on separate authority. — [Nventory](https://nventory.io/blog/2026-us-tariff-changes-ecommerce-sellers); [DoDropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/dropshipping-tariffs/)
- De minimis was suspended for all countries on Aug 29, 2025. Every parcel now needs a customs entry. — [Korea Import Hub](https://www.koreaimporthub.com/article/kbeauty-cosmetics)
- July 24, 2026: a new Section 301 tariff took effect on products of 60 economies, at 12.5% for China and most others. The same Q4 2026 guide still lists beauty tools and pet products among top dropship sellers. — [Crevio](https://crevio.co/blog/best-dropshipping-products)
- Section 122 tariffs lapsed July 24, 2026. — [CalcMyTariff](https://www.calcmytariff.com/country/china/pet-products); [Korea Import Hub](https://www.koreaimporthub.com/article/kbeauty-cosmetics)
- Pet products from China: ~41.5% total effective tariff (MFN 4% + Section 301 25% + forced-labor Section 301 12.5%). — [CalcMyTariff](https://www.calcmytariff.com/country/china/pet-products)
- Beauty: one margin analysis puts 2026 beauty tariffs at 20–25%, and says formulations made in China or Korea face meaningful duty increases. — [Nummbas](https://www.nummbas.com/blog/tariffs-ecommerce-margins-2026/). This conflicts with dropship blogs calling beauty/pet "low-exposure" at 5–7.5% Section 301 rates. — [DoDropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/dropshipping-tariffs/)
- Korea: after the Feb 2026 IEEPA ruling, Korea's rate fell to 10% under Section 122 (expired July 2026). Since July 24, 2026, Section 301 tops Korean goods up to a 12.5% total, and this is being challenged at the Court of International Trade while duties are still collected. — [Korea Import Hub](https://www.koreaimporthub.com/article/kbeauty-cosmetics); [Meamo](https://meamoshop.com/guides/wholesale/korean-import-tariffs-aesthetic-products/). Another guide cites a combined 15% under the Korea framework (Aug 2026). — [KoalaGains HTS Ch.33](https://koalagains.com/industry-tariff-report/chapters/33-essential-oils-and-cosmetics/tariff-updates)
- 2025 effects (older data): Olive Young imposed a 15% customs duty on all US orders, and some carriers charged a $80 flat per-package fee during the transition. Economists warned of higher prices, fewer varieties and slower delivery for K-beauty. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/asian-america/end-de-minimis-exemption-tariffs-korean-beauty-products-rcna228929); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/01/nx-s1-5519724/korean-skincare-industry-expected-to-be-hit-hard-by-end-of-de-minimus-exemption); [UCSD](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/cleansed-toned-and-tariffed-whats-happening-to-k-beauty-in-the-u.s)
- LED masks: Chinese OEMs (e.g., Shenzhen Desida) hold FDA 510(k) clearances, indicating China is a primary production base for consumer LED masks. — [FDA K254079](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K254079.pdf)

**EU/UK note**
- EU GPSR Art. 16(1) (applicable since Dec 13, 2024) bars placing products on the EU market without an EU-established responsible economic operator. Classic China-to-EU-consumer dropshipping usually makes the lister the importer. — [O1.eu](https://o1.eu/news-insights/gpsr-eu-2023-988-marketplace-sellers-responsible-person-2026); [GPSRready](https://gpsrready.pl/en/blog/gpsr-dropshipping-allegro)
- Marketplace enforcement: bol.com made manufacturer data mandatory from Mar 5, 2026 and responsible-operator data from Apr 1, 2026, and won't publish offers without them. ### Inferences
- **Most tariff-exposed (China-made, stacked 301 duties):** pet hardgoods (beds, toys, feeders, litter boxes); electronic beauty and wellness devices (LED masks, hair dryers/stylers, massage guns, microcurrent tools, smart feeders); press-on nails and nail tools; GLP-1 pen cases; and most textile wellness goods (sleep masks, bonnets, cooling pillows). Exact rates depend on HTS classification. Some consumer electronics have historically had different 301 list treatment, so verify per HTS code.
- **Commonly made outside China:** K-beauty (Korea; ~12.5–15% exposure), J-beauty (Japan; similar new-301 exposure), dietary supplements (largely US contract manufacturers, so domestic-origin product avoids import duty; this is general knowledge, not sourced here), some pet treats/chews (US, Thailand), textiles/beds (Vietnam, India, Bangladesh increasingly), and Laifen-type hair tools (Chinese brand; origin China). Under the July 2026 rule, a 12.5% floor reportedly applies to most of 60 economies, so "made outside China" reduces but does not eliminate exposure.
- **Practical model shift:** with de minimis gone, the economics favor US-warehoused inventory (bulk-imported, duties paid once) over parcel-by-parcel cross-border dropshipping, especially for low-AOV items where per-parcel entry fees and duties eat margin.
- **For EU/UK sales,** pure China-to-consumer dropshipping is effectively non-compliant without an EU responsible person under GPSR. Cosmetics additionally require an EU/UK Responsible Person and CPNP/SCPN notification (general knowledge; not sourced in this pass).
- **[EU/UK background, unverified in session] EU low-value import changes:**
  - The EU removed the €22 VAT exemption in July 2021 (IOSS), so VAT is already due on every parcel.
  - EU institutions agreed in late 2025 to abolish the €150 customs-duty exemption. An interim flat customs duty of about €3 per item on low-value e-commerce parcels was slated to apply from 1 July 2026, ahead of the EU Customs Data Hub (~2028). A separate per-parcel handling fee was also proposed.
  - If in force, this hits low-AOV China-direct parcels (mouth tape, press-on nails, pet toys) hardest. Verify the current status and date.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified] UK:**
  - The UK announced it will end the £135 low-value customs-duty relief (by 2029 at the latest); VAT is already collected at point of sale.
  - The Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 gives the government powers to impose product-safety duties on online marketplaces. GB general product safety law and OPSS enforcement apply in the meantime.
  - Details and commencement dates for 2026 were not verified.
- **[EU/UK background, unverified] EPR is a Europe-specific dropship barrier.** Germany (VerpackG/LUCID packaging register, ElektroG for electronics) and France (Triman/AGEC, multiple EPR streams) require per-country producer registration. Marketplaces (Amazon.de/.fr) block non-registered sellers. This affects nearly every category in these notes.
- **Tariffs in Europe vs. the US:** the US tariff stack (Section 301 on China plus the new 12.5% Section 301) has no direct EU/UK equivalent. EU/UK duties on these goods are standard MFN rates (low single digits for many cosmetics and plastic/textile goods; higher for some apparel/textiles). For China-made goods, Europe may therefore be *less* tariff-exposed than the US. The binding constraints in Europe are compliance (GPSR/RP/CPNP/EPR) and the end of low-value relief, not tariffs. This is an inference from the sourced US tariff data plus the background above.
