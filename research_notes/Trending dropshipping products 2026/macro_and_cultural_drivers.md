## Q1. Current US and global consumer conditions (inflation, tariffs, sentiment, jobs, K-shape, trade-down, dupes, BNPL)

### Takeaway
The US consumer in November 2026 is spending in nominal terms but under real strain. An Iran-war energy shock (from about March 2026) pushed headline CPI to about 3.7% YoY in September. Wages are growing only about 3.1%, sentiment hit a record low in May and is only slowly recovering, a ~10-12.5% broad Section 301 tariff layer is in place, and the Fed is leaning toward hikes. Spending is concentrated in high-income households; low- and middle-income shoppers are trading down, buying dupes and private label, and using BNPL more.

**Tariffs (legal regime as of the latest data found)**
- On 20 Feb 2026 the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in *Learning Resources v. Trump* that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. This ended the "reciprocal" and fentanyl IEEPA tariffs. Section 232 tariffs and existing Section 301 tariffs (e.g., on China) were unaffected — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/us-supreme-court-holds-ieepa-does-not-authorize-tariffs); [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/us-supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs.html)
- The administration replaced them with a **Section 122 global tariff of 10%** effective 24 Feb 2026 for 150 days. An increase to 15% was announced, and sources conflict on whether 10% or 15% applied — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/02/trump-temp-global-tariff-after-scotus-ieepa-ruling); [RILA](https://www.rila.org/blog/2026/05/how-is-the-administration-using-section-122)
- Section 122 **expired 24 Jul 2026**. New **Section 301 tariffs of 10% or 12.5%** replaced it the same day, covering products from about 80 countries (about 99.4% of US import value). They have no statutory expiry and are in litigation. One importer guide says most importers pay "roughly the same". Analysts warn that stacking could push some rates above 20% — [BorderBuddy](https://borderbuddy.com/trade-tariff-news/section-122-is-out-section-301-is-in); [Fennemore](https://www.fennemorelaw.com/new-u-s-tariffs-replace-expiring-section-122-tariffs/); [Strix](https://www.strixsmart.com/section-122-surcharge)
- IEEPA tariff refunds are unresolved. SCOTUS left the question to lower courts, more than 1,000 companies have sued, and refunds could take years — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/2/20/tariff-refunds-could-take-years-amid-us-supreme-court-ruling-experts-warn)
- Deloitte's holiday survey: **79%** of consumers are bracing for higher prices this year — [Deloitte press release](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/deloitte-holiday-spending-set-to-increase.html); [Retail Touchpoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/?p=621781)

### Inferences
- Real (inflation-adjusted) consumer purchasing power is roughly flat to negative for the median household: wages +3.1% vs CPI 3.7%, plus a gasoline tax-like drag of hundreds of dollars per household. Nominal sales growth in 2026 is materially flattered by price increases.
- For dropshippers, the tariff regime is no longer the 2025 IEEPA chaos. It is now a broad, persistent ~10-12.5% Section 301 layer, plus China-specific 301s and Section 232 metals duties. This raises landed costs on nearly all imported goods and keeps retail price points creeping up, which reinforces the dupe and value positioning of most dropship assortments.
- Value-seeking has spread up the income ladder: 55+ dupe buyers, and higher-income shoppers buying private label. "Premium look at a lower price" (dupe or alternative) products have a wider addressable market than in prior years.
- A Fed tilted toward hikes, plus rising BNPL reliance among middle- and lower-income shoppers, means credit-funded holiday spending carries late-Q1 2027 payback risk (returns, chargebacks, weaker January).

## Q2. 2026 holiday season forecasts (growth, categories, channels) and early-2027 outlook

### Takeaway
Every major forecaster expects the strongest nominal holiday growth in years: NRF +4.2% to about $1.013T, Mastercard +5.5%, Adobe online +6.7% to $275.1B, and Deloitte e-commerce +7.5-8.4%. Roughly half of that growth is price inflation. Online and mobile share keeps rising, AI-referred traffic is exploding, and electronics, toys and video games lead categories. Shopping is pulled forward (October Prime event) and back-loaded to Thanksgiving weekend.

### Inferences
- Real holiday unit growth is likely only ~2-3%: NRF says about half of the 4.2% is real, and Mastercard says about half of 5.5% is price. Winners will be products that feel like a deal at gift price points, not broad lifts across all categories.
- Online growth (Mastercard +11%, Adobe +6.7%, Deloitte +7.5-8.4%) far outpaces total retail, and mobile carries about 57%. Mobile-first creative, short-form video and social commerce (growing 9x faster per Salesforce) are the key acquisition channels for dropshippers this season.
- Shopping is bimodal: early buyers in October (Prime) and a large Thanksgiving-weekend cohort (64%). For dropshippers, the critical window is 20 Nov-2 Dec, followed by shipping-cutoff risk for overseas fulfillment in mid-December.
- Electronics and gaming (Mastercard +10.7% YTD; Prime-event video games +90%) and toys (+158%) are the strongest category signals. Accessories and adjacent low-ticket items in these ecosystems are the dropship-friendly angle.
- Early 2027 looks softer: possible Fed hikes, BNPL repayments, tariff persistence and still-elevated energy costs. January demand may skew to "resolution" categories (GLP-1-adjacent wellness, fitness, Dry January/NA) and value. Divided government makes major new tariff legislation unlikely, but executive tariff actions and litigation continue.

## Q5. How AI is changing shopping behavior and product discovery in 2026

### Takeaway
2026 is the first holiday season where AI assistants are a mainstream discovery channel. Adobe forecasts +130% YoY AI-referred retail traffic, Salesforce says ~20% of holiday e-commerce traffic will originate from AI agents, and Bain finds 24% of online shoppers will start holiday shopping on AI platforms. But **agent-completed checkout remains a low single-digit share** and trust is low (~16%). Amazon and eBay are blocking third-party agents, while OpenAI, Walmart and Shopify embrace them.

### Cited Findings
- Adobe: AI-referred traffic to US retail sites **+130% YoY** this season, with peaks on Thanksgiving (+159%), Black Friday (+95%) and Cyber Monday (+88%) — [Adobe](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/09/adobe-us-holiday-shopping-season-to-hit-record); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/ecommerce-marketing/adobe-2026-holiday-forecast-275-billion-ai-traffic-130-percent/). - OpenAI Instant Checkout runs on the Agentic Commerce Protocol, and **Walmart** lets customers buy inside ChatGPT. ### Inferences
- AI assistants are now a discovery layer that independent stores can reach. Clean product feeds, structured data, honest specs, reviews and enrollment in agentic-checkout programs (Shopify Agentic, OpenAI ACP) are new acquisition levers.
- AI comparison shopping punishes undifferentiated, overpriced dropship listings: agents surface price and spec comparisons instantly. Margin must come from bundling, branding, unique angles or speed, not price opacity.
- A narrower traffic-to-order conversion (Salesforce) suggests more research-stage browsing. Retargeting and clear value messaging matter more.

## Q6. What consumers are cutting back on vs still splurging on

### Takeaway
Consumers are cutting fuel (lower-income), snacks and fast food (GLP-1 households), alcohol, and discretionary big-ticket items (UK especially), and are trading down broadly via dupes and private label. They are still spending on electronics and gaming, toys, social and celebratory moments (World Cup, July 4 food), pets, wellness and self-care, and experiences (often BNPL-financed). High-income households and Gen Z are the main "splurgers". "Little treat" spending is strongest among the most financially anxious.

### Inferences
- The splurge/save line runs through categories more than through income. Shoppers economize on staples, fuel and commodity goods (trading down to dupes and private label) to fund small, emotionally resonant purchases: tech accessories, gaming, toys for kids, self-care, pets, hobbies and comfort items. This fits dropship price points ($15-$60 "affordable treat" gifts) well.
- Gen Z plus millennials (50% of holiday spend), high earners and the 55+ dupe-hunter are three distinct targetable segments with different value messages: trend and identity, quality and premium, and "same look for less".
- Big-ticket discretionary goods are vulnerable, especially in the UK and among the US middle-income cohort. Low-ticket, giftable, impulse-friendly items with strong visual demos for short-form video should outperform.

- No 2026 survey quantifying "little treat" or affordable-luxury spending was found. That support is inferred from McKinsey's late-2025 splurge data.
