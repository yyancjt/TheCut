## 1. Emergency preparedness, resilience and backup power: is demand rising, and why? (US + EU/UK)

### Takeaway
2026 had strong drivers for preparedness demand:
- **War and fuel prices.** The US/Israel–Iran war started in late Feb 2026, the Strait of Hormuz was effectively closed, crude passed $100 and US gasoline rose about 30–50%.
- **Outages.** Winter Storm Fern knocked out power to more than 1M customers. Hurricane Isaias knocked out more than 900k.
- **Electricity costs.** US residential electricity prices kept rising, partly because of data-center load.
- **Government guidance.** In Europe, the EU's 72-hour self-sufficiency guidance (formal guidelines published 3 Nov 2026) and an energy-price shock in the UK added to demand.

Hard sell-through data is scarce. Signals are search and marketplace spikes (Jungle Scout, Google), anecdotal retailer surges and wide-ranging market forecasts. The best dropshipping fits are light, battery-free or small-battery items: water filters, first-aid and 72-hour kit components, crank/solar radios, solar lights, mylar blankets. Large power stations are heavy, count as dangerous goods for shipping and are dominated by major brands.

### Inferences
- The demand drivers are real but episodic. Each spike (war news, storm warnings, viral rumors, EU guidance) is short, so a dropshipper with 7–15-day shipping times will often miss the peak. Evergreen positioning works better: "72-hour kit to EU/Swedish/German guidance," a hurricane or winter-storm checklist bundle, or a "car kit."
- **Best-fit SKUs.** These are light, cheap, battery-free or small-installed-battery, and low-return:
  - Personal water filter straws and bottles, purification tablets, collapsible water containers
  - Mylar blankets, ponchos, N95/P100 masks
  - First-aid kit refills and IFAKs
  - Hand-crank/solar weather radios (NOAA band in the US; FM/DAB in Europe)
  - Solar lanterns and headlamps, waterproof document pouches
  - Pre-packed 72-hour grab bags with EU-guideline checklists (sold as a kit to raise AOV above the de minimis/flat-duty thresholds; see section 9)
- **Poor fits.** Large portable power stations (>100 Wh) and solar generators count as dangerous goods for air shipping, are heavy and expensive to return, and appear dominated by EcoFlow, Jackery, Anker and BLUETTI. Brand dominance is inferred from the search results being mostly brand blogs, not measured. Generic power banks are also risky given the recall wave (Casely, INIU, VEEKTOMX) and marketplace scrutiny.
- **Gas masks.** Jungle Scout shows demand, but they carry claims/certification risk and reputational risk. Treat with caution.
- **Energy prices as a second, slower driver.** Higher US and UK electricity prices support "energy-saving" products (see section 2: heated blankets, draught excluders, smart plugs). In the UK, the Jan 2027 cap rise and the VAT relief ending in Mar 2027 create a Q1 2027 messaging window.

### Gaps
- No audited 2026 unit or revenue data for power stations, generators, radios or kits in the US or EU. The market reports conflict wildly.
- Wildfire season 2026 (US West, Canada, Mediterranean) and its effect on demand were not quantified.
- The European portable power station / balcony solar (Germany "Balkonkraftwerk") market and the effect of the April 2025 Iberian blackout on Spain/Portugal demand were not researched (search budget exhausted).
- Gas-mask/respirator certification rules for EU (EN 14387) and US (NIOSH) were not verified this session.

## 2. Climate/heat and air-quality products: neck fans, cooling towels, portable AC, air purifiers, dehumidifiers, smoke; plus winter "heat the person" products

### Takeaway
Summer 2026 heat drove the strongest measurable cooling demand in Europe:
- Western Europe air-conditioner sales were up more than 70% YoY in H1, per one report.
- Portable ACs sold out in Germany and Austria.
- Handheld and neck fans sold out in London.

US evidence is anecdotal (hardware-store sell-outs). Air purifiers have structural demand from wildfire smoke, but hard 2026 data is missing. For Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, the Northern Hemisphere opportunity flips to low-wattage personal heating (heated blankets, throws, heated apparel), driven by energy-price shocks, especially in the UK. Australia's summer is the counter-seasonal market for cooling items.

### Inferences
- **Good fits for summer 2027 (Europe and US) and for AU/NZ now:**
  - Cooling towels: no battery, very light, near-zero return rate, the best pure dropshipping fit.
  - Neck fans and clip-on handheld fans: small installed batteries, acceptable under carrier rules for batteries contained in equipment.
  - Solar fan hats, cooling bed sheets and pillow covers, misting bottles, blackout and thermal curtains.
- **Poor fits:** portable ACs and large fans are heavy and bulky, have high return and damage rates, need EU and UK plug variants, and must clear EU appliance energy-label and CE rules.
- **Air purifier replacement filters** are a recurring-revenue, lightweight angle. Naming "compatible with [brand]" carries trademark sensitivity. Whole purifiers face CE/UKCA, ozone and CARB rules (California ionizer/ozone limits; not verified this session).
- **Q4 2026 to Q1 2027 (UK/EU focus):** "heat the person, not the house" products: USB/low-voltage heated throws, heated insoles, heated vests (battery packs mean shipping restrictions), draught excluders, thermal underwear, hot-water-bottle covers. The UK cap decision (by 25 Nov) and the Jan 2027 increase are natural campaign hooks. Mains-powered heated blankets need UK plugs (Type G) and EU plugs (Type C/F), plus electrical safety certification. That is a material compliance burden for a dropshipper.

### Gaps
- No full-season 2026 US cooling sales data (Circana, NIQ, GfK) was found. The Europe figures come mostly from Chinese export and state-media framing.
- UK heatwave retail data for 2026 (Argos, John Lewis fan sales) was not researched (search budget exhausted).
- Dehumidifier demand (UK damp/mould, heat-pump households) is not covered.
- CARB and EU ozone-generator rules for air purifiers were not verified.

## 3. Home: small kitchen appliances, home café, cleaning gadgets, cozy decor, hosting, organization, smart home, renters' upgrades

### Takeaway
Home is a slow-growth but steady category:
- Circana forecasts US home products demand up 2.4% in 2026 and 2.8% in 2027. Another Circana adviser expects units to be slightly down in 2026.
- Small appliances benefit from "affordable joy" buys (ice-cream makers, air fryers) and a replacement cycle for 2020-era purchases.
- The strongest macro hook is coffee inflation pushing home café setups. US retail coffee was up 18.3% YoY in Jan 2026, 12% of US households owned an espresso machine in 2025 (vs 8% in 2020), and UK home coffee-machine purchases were up 43% on Black Friday 2025.

Decor is moving to warm, colorful, "Grandmacore" and maximalist looks (Pinterest: Glamoratti, Afrohemian, '80s, circus). The best dropshipping fits are light accessories around these trends, not the appliances themselves (Ninja, Nespresso and De'Longhi dominate those).

## 9. Tariff exposure, regulation, shipping and IP risk: what is most exposed, what is made outside China, and EU/UK-specific compliance

### Takeaway
**US:**
- The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs, and collection stopped 24 Feb 2026; refunds are flowing (Nintendo got $300M).
- The Section 122 10% surcharge expired 24 Jul 2026.
- What remains is Section 301 on China (electronics about 37.5% effective) and Section 232 metals, which since 6 Apr 2026 applies to the full customs value of covered derivatives (household goods at 25%; appliances added at 50% in 2025).
- De minimis is gone for all countries, which hits ultra-low-AOV dropshipping hardest.

**EU:**
- The €150 duty exemption ended 1 Jul 2026, replaced by a flat €3 duty per item (until 1 Jul 2028) plus a €2 handling fee from 1 Nov 2026.
- GPSR requires an EU-based Responsible Person, and marketplaces are enforcing it.
- The EU Battery Regulation requires user-replaceable portable batteries in devices from 18 Feb 2027; power banks are excluded.

Lithium batteries constrain international mail. USPS accepts only batteries installed in equipment, and standalone batteries go via dangerous-goods courier. Most metal-heavy (fitness weights, cookware, appliances) and electronics products are the most tariff-exposed. No reliable data was found on which trending SKUs are made outside China.

**EU/UK customs and compliance**
- EU €150 customs duty exemption abolished 1 Jul 2026. Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382 introduces a temporary flat €3 customs duty per item (distance sales, consignments ≤€150) until 1 Jul 2028. Worked example: 5 T-shirts = €3 (one item type); 1 T-shirt + 1 watch = €6. Sources differ on whether "item" means tariff line. — [Baker McKenzie, 2 Jul 2026](https://globalimportblog.bakermckenzie.com/2026/07/02/eu-low-value-consignment-relief-abolished-and-eur-3-customs-duty-introduced-from-1-july-2026/); [European Commission VAT OSS guidance](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/eur-3-customs-duty-vat-guidelines-2026-06-16_en); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/blog/eu-end-150-customs-duty-exemption-2026.html)

- GPSR (Regulation (EU) 2023/988):
  - Non-EU sellers need an EU-established Responsible Person (Art. 16). Chinese factories generally won't act as one, so third-party RP services or EU fulfilment partners are used.
  - Amazon, bol.com, Otto, Kaufland and others require GPSR attributes. Marketplaces must suspend repeat non-compliant traders after warning.
  - In 2026, authorities increasingly demand technical documentation directly.
  - Dropshippers are "part of the supply chain."
  - Sources: [FiveX](https://website.fivex.com/blog/gtm-gpsr-explained-chinese-sellers-manufacturers/); [Spocket](https://www.spocket.co/es/blogs/cumplimiento-de-la-normativa-de-seguridad-de-productos-de-la-ue); [ExamineChina](https://www.examinechina.com/gpsr-importing-from-china/); [Nemko](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/general-product-safety-regulation-gspr) (mostly vendor sources)

### Inferences

**Tariff and regulation exposure ranking for trending categories (US):**
1. Steel/aluminum-heavy goods (dumbbells, kettlebells, weight plates, rucking plates, metal camping cookware, appliances, metal furniture, e-bike frames): highest. Section 232 now on full value (25–50%), plus China 301.
2. Chinese electronics (projectors, retro handhelds, power stations, fans, heated apparel controllers): high, at about 25–37.5%+. Some ITA duty-free items are an exception.
3. Lithium-battery goods: tariff plus dangerous-goods shipping plus recall/CPSC risk.
4. Plastic/textile soft goods (bag charms, plush, craft kits, TCG sleeves, cooling towels, Pilates props, jewelry): lower absolute duty per unit. Still subject to MFN plus 301 on China and the loss of de minimis, but low unit values keep landed-cost impact manageable when bundled.

**EU/UK economics:**
- The €3 per item flat duty plus €2 handling fee (a €5 floor per item type) penalizes sub-€15 single-item orders. Bundles (kits, multi-packs of the same item type) and AOVs of €25+ absorb it better.
- Practical implication: sell kits, such as "72-hour kit," "crochet starter kit," "Pilates home kit," "TCG collector kit," or "home café kit," rather than single low-cost SKUs. The same logic applies in the US after de minimis.
- GPSR makes "no-EU-entity" China-direct dropshipping to EU consumers increasingly non-viable on major marketplaces. Budget for an EU Responsible Person, product-safety files, CE marking where applicable (electricals, toys) and WEEE/battery EPR registrations (not verified this session).
- The UK is outside GPSR but has its own regime (UKCA/CE, UK product safety). The UK's low-value import relief status for 2026 was not verified.

**Battery-free is a strong selection filter for both regions.** Among trending items, the clean fits are:
- Cooling towels

- Pilates and resistance props
