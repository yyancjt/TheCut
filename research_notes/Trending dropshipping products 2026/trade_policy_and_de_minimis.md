## 1. Current US tariff status (China and other sourcing countries): IEEPA, SCOTUS ruling, Section 122, Section 301, Section 232, US–China truce, refunds

### Takeaway
All IEEPA tariffs are gone. That covers the "reciprocal" tariffs and the fentanyl tariffs, which the Supreme Court struck down 6-3 on 20 Feb 2026, and CBP stopped collecting them on 24 Feb 2026. A temporary 10% Section 122 global surcharge filled the gap from 24 Feb to 24 Jul 2026. Since 24 Jul 2026 a "forced-labor" Section 301 tariff has applied: 10% or 12.5% on 60 economies, covering about 99.4% of US imports. China-origin goods still carry the legacy Section 301 duties (25% on Lists 1–3, 7.5% on List 4A) on top of the new 12.5%. The US–China truce, now extended to 10 Jan 2027, mostly keeps the remaining *China-specific* escalations frozen: port fees, the Section 301 exclusions, and China's rare-earth controls and retaliatory tariffs. A cluster of deadlines on 9–10 Jan 2027 is the next cliff.

### Cited Findings
**Supreme Court / IEEPA (superseded regime)**
- On 20 Feb 2026 the Supreme Court decided *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*, consolidated with *Trump v. V.O.S. Selections*. It held 6-3 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. Chief Justice Roberts wrote the majority/plurality, and the reasoning was fractured. Justice Kavanaugh dissented, warning of a "chaotic" refund situation. The Court affirmed in V.O.S. and vacated/remanded *Learning Resources* for dismissal on jurisdictional grounds. Roberts wrote: "There is no exception to the major questions doctrine for emergency statutes." — [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/2/supreme-court-decides-learning-resources-inc-v-trump-and-trump-v-vos-selections-inc); [Current Federal Tax Developments](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/2/20/supreme-court-invalidates-executive-tariffs-under-ieepa-a-technical-analysis-of-learning-resources-inc-v-trump); [Nat'l Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/summary-supreme-court-decision-ieepa-tariffs)
- CBP stopped collecting the IEEPA-based fentanyl and reciprocal duties on 24 Feb 2026. Older guides that still list a 20% (or 10%) fentanyl layer on China are out of date. — [Customs-Lookup: China import duty stack](https://www.customs-lookup.com/tariff-changes/china-import-duty-stack/)
- Context, now superseded: IEEPA "reciprocal" rates had been very high. Vietnam's, for example, was announced at 46% and later set at 20%. Vietnam has since moved to 10% under Section 122 and then 12.5% under forced-labor Section 301. — [Tariffstool: Vietnam](https://www.tariffstool.com/tariffs-from-vietnam); [Airlift USA: Vietnam](https://airliftusa.com/tariff-updates/vietnam)

**Section 122 bridge tariff (expired)**
- CBP began collecting a 10% Section 122 global surcharge at 12:01 a.m. ET on 24 Feb 2026. It hit its 150-day statutory end on 24 Jul 2026. — [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/us-tariff-update-section-122-duties-found-unauthorized-by-law-ieepa-refunds-under-way); [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/section-122-tariffs-refunds-trade-policy)
- On 7 May 2026 the Court of International Trade (CIT) held the Section 122 tariffs unlawful but limited relief to three plaintiff importers. On 11 Jun 2026 the Federal Circuit stayed that ruling, so collection continued until expiry. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/us-tariff-update-section-122-duties-found-unauthorized-by-law-ieepa-refunds-under-way)
- Section 122 duties cannot be refunded through CBP's CAPE process. As of 12 Oct 2026 the appeal was still pending and no Section 122 refunds were payable. Importers are advised to file protests within 180 days of liquidation. — [Gateway Lines](https://gatewaylines.com/press-releases/section-122-tariff-refunds-who-gets-money-back); [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/tariffs-redux-what-importers-should-know-about-ieepa-refunds-and-section-122/)

**IEEPA refunds**
- Refunds are not automatic. Importers must file a CAPE Declaration in ACE. CBP says refunds "will generally be issued within 60–90 days following acceptance." — [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/tariffs-redux-what-importers-should-know-about-ieepa-refunds-and-section-122/); [Great Lakes Customs Law](https://greatlakescustomslaw.com/ieepa-tariff-refunds/)
- Refund progress:
  - As of 10 Jul 2026, per a CBP court filing as reported: $86.3B repaid and $121.75B accepted. — [Tariff Bureau updates](https://tariffbureau.com/updates)
  - As of 2 Oct 2026: about $136.6B in claims accepted and about $126B sent to Treasury for payment. "Sent to Treasury" is not the same as received by the importer. The figure came from a search summary that cited this group of refund-tracker pages; the exact originating page is not confirmed. — [Western Overseas](https://www.westernoverseas.com/ieepa-tariff-refunds/); [Airlift USA refunds](https://airliftusa.com/tariff-updates/tariff-refunds)
- CAPE Phase 3 launched on 6 Oct 2026. It opens refunds of *finally liquidated* IEEPA duties to importers that are plaintiffs in pending CIT cases. — [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/a-capeable-solution-how-we-can-help-with-cbps-cape-phase-3-rollout-for-cit-plaintiffs-seeking-ieepa-tariff-refunds/)
- Implication for dropshippers: the IEEPA duties a small seller paid were often paid through the platform or courier under DDP, or by consumers at the door. Getting those back depends on who was importer of record. This is an inference; see Inferences.

**Forced-labor Section 301 (current replacement layer, in force since 24 Jul 2026)**
- USTR imposed new Section 301 tariffs on 60 economies for failing to prohibit or enforce bans on forced-labor imports. The action covers about 99.4% of US imports and took effect 24 Jul 2026, the same moment Section 122 expired, so there was no gap. — [USTR press release, Jul 2026](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations); [Thomson Reuters Tax](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/blog/60-economies-two-tariff-tiers-and-one-deadline-thats-already-passed-is-your-trade-team-ready-for-the-next-wave/); [Federal Register notice 2026-15181 (28 Jul 2026)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies)
- Tiers and rates:
  - Flat 10%: includes UK, India, Mexico and Canada.
  - Flat 12.5%: 38 economies, including **China, Vietnam, Brazil and Russia**.
  - EU and Taiwan: capped at 10% *net of MFN*.
  - Japan, South Korea and Switzerland: capped at 12.5% net of MFN.
  - For China and Brazil, the new duty applies *in addition to* existing Section 301 duties.
  - One source gives the citation as 91 FR 47318.
  - — [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/forced-labour-section-301-final-action); [Zonos docs](https://zonos.com/docs/guides/country-guides/united-states/section-301-forced-labor); [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/forced-labor-section-301-tariffs-imposed-to-replace-expiring-section-122-tariffs-what-importers-need-to-know)
- USMCA-qualifying goods from Canada and Mexico are not subject to the forced-labor 301 tariff. — [International Trade Insights](https://www.internationaltradeinsights.com/2026/07/ustr-imposes-new-section-301-tariffs-targeting-forced-labor-concerns-across-60-economies/)
- Exemptions are set at HTS-line level. Global Trade Alert counts only 863 of 2,120 Annex II tariff codes as exempt as entered. — [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/forced-labour-section-301-final-action)

### Inferences
- For a typical China-sourced dropship SKU that is not a 232 good, the current US duty is roughly: MFN + 25% (Lists 1–3) or 7.5% (List 4A) + 12.5% forced-labor 301. That puts a typical List 3 item at about 37.5% plus MFN. This is lower than the April–November 2025 peak, when IEEPA fentanyl and reciprocal layers stacked on top. However, since de minimis has ended, every parcel now pays it.
- Vietnam, India and other non-China origins now differ from China mainly by the legacy China Section 301 layer of 7.5–25 points. That is a smaller gap than under IEEPA in 2025, when China peaked far higher, but it persists.
- Legal-durability risk has moved from IEEPA to Section 301 and Section 232, which courts have historically upheld. The forced-labor 301 is the new litigation target. Next decision points: 9–10 Jan 2027 (truce, exclusions, port fees and rare-earth deadlines); the excess-capacity 301 outcome; and 28 Feb 2027 (Vietnam IP 301).
- Dropshippers who were importer of record under DDP during the IEEPA period may be owed refunds. Those who bought DDP through platforms or couriers likely are not. This is not verified.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Trump's widely reported intention to raise Section 122 from 10% to 15% was ever implemented. All legal/CBP-oriented sources found say 10% was collected. Some dropshipping blogs in the results cite "15%".
- Exact 2024 four-year-review China Section 301 increases still in force in 2026 (e.g., lithium-ion non-EV batteries, semiconductors, medical gloves) were not verified in this session.
- Total Section 122 duties collected: one search summary cited roughly $25B from an unclear source. Not verified.
- I found no source on whether any tariff action targeting China specifically took effect on 10 Nov 2026. The truce extension appears to have prevented changes.
- The Federal Circuit outcome on Section 122 and any court challenges to the forced-labor 301 had no reported resolution as of mid-Nov 2026.

## 2. US de minimis ($800): China/HK end (May 2025), global suspension (Aug 2025), statutory repeal, postal taxation, and effects on landed cost/delivery

### Takeaway
US de minimis is effectively dead for every origin and every mode:
- 2 May 2025: ended for China/HK.
- 29 Aug 2025: suspended globally.
- July 2027: statutory repeal under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA).

The suspension survived the SCOTUS IEEPA ruling. CBP re-based it in June 2026 interim final rules, and the CIT upheld it on 13 Aug 2026 in *Axle of Dearborn*. Postal shipments now pay full ad valorem duty only; the per-package specific-duty option ended 28 Feb 2026. They also need full HTS-level data under a new postal informal entry process, which CBP has proposed replacing with formal "Entry Type 13". For China-direct small-parcel dropshipping, every order now carries the full duty stack plus brokerage/compliance cost. That has pushed the model toward bulk import into US warehouses.

- Platform response: large platforms moved to collecting duties at checkout (DDP) during 2025. Their logistics chains enter goods through commercial carriers, not the postal network. — [ChinaSpreadsheet](https://chinaspreadsheet.org/us-customs-2026/)
- Landed-cost examples. All come from vendor/affiliate sources, so treat them as illustrative:
  - An AliExpress item landed at about $17.77 in May 2026 against about $7.50 before the change. — [SaleHoo](https://www.salehoo.com/learn/directories-for-the-best-dropshipping-wholesale-suppliers)
  - Doba's example puts China-direct landed cost at $17.60 at a 7.5% rate and $19.00 at 25%, against $18.50 from a US-warehouse supplier. On 25%-list goods, US stock is cheaper even before counting slower delivery and harder returns. — [Doba](https://www.doba.com/blog/start-dropshipping/what-is-dropshipping/dropshipping-from-china-in-2026-tariffs--us-options-37298)
  - A $30 product's landed cost rose to about $42.79 from about $36.04 before 2026, using a 22.5% duty assumption. — vendor blog cited in search ([Kinja ecommerce](https://kinja.com/ecommerce/is-dropshipping-worth-it-after-tariffs-2026); exact page not confirmed)
- Demand effect:
  - After de minimis ended, Temu's US audience fell by as much as 62% and Shein's by 47%.
  - As of April 2026 both were still about 30% below pre-tariff levels, with recovery aided by US warehousing.
  - — [Measure Protocol](https://www.measureprotocol.com/insights/shein-temu-shoppers-after-us-tariffs)
- Fulfillment constraint: China-direct parcels rarely meet TikTok Shop US dispatch and tracking requirements. — [Easync](https://easync.io/articles/aliexpress-for-dropshipping/)

### Inferences
- The economics of a China-direct parcel now cost: full duty stack (about 37.5% plus MFN for many China goods) + entry-filing/brokerage cost per parcel + DDP-collection friction. Low-AOV (under about $20) impulse products were the core of AliExpress dropshipping, and this structure hits them hardest.
- Postal mail was the cheapest China-direct channel (ePacket-style). It now carries data and filing burdens, ET13 if finalized, that are hard for small sellers. Expect fewer postal options and more consolidated commercial or B2B2C entry by platforms and 3PLs.
- Delivery times: I found no hard 2026 data on China-direct US transit times. However, routing via customs-cleared bulk to US warehouses (2–5 day domestic delivery) has become the default. See Q5.

### Gaps
- Final compliance date for postal informal entry / ET13: sources conflict between 24 Jul 2026, 22 Oct 2026, 20 Jan 2027 and 22 Sep 2026. Not resolved from primary text.
- No measured 2026 data on China-to-US parcel volumes, postal suspension by foreign posts, or average transit times. The 2025 suspension of US-bound service by many foreign postal operators was not re-verified in this session.
- Whether the *Axle of Dearborn* ruling has been appealed is unknown.

## 3. EU and UK customs changes (co-primary market): €150 duty exemption removal, flat per-item duty, handling fee, IOSS/VAT, UK £135 relief review

### Takeaway
The EU has abolished in practice its €150 customs-duty exemption for e-commerce.
- **€3 flat customs duty** since **1 Jul 2026**. It is charged per item/tariff category in parcels under €150 and is temporary until about 1 Jul 2028.
- **€2 handling fee** since **1 Nov 2026**. It is charged per item, applies at any value, and is non-refundable.
- The Council gave final approval to the customs reform in Sep 2026.
- From about 2028 the Customs Data Hub will replace the flat duty with normal classification-based duties.

The UK has confirmed it will remove its £135 Low Value Import duty relief by **October 2028 at the latest**, brought forward from March 2029. Duty will move to quarterly payment after entry. Until then UK consignments of £135 or less remain customs-duty-free. VAT has been collected at point of sale since 2021; see Gaps.

For a cheap multi-item China-direct order to the EU, the new fixed charges (€3 + €2 per item) can rival or exceed the product cost of a €5–10 item. Shein and Temu pre-positioned stock in EU 3PLs before July 2026.

### Cited Findings
**EU flat €3 duty (in force since 1 Jul 2026)**
- Non-EU parcels worth under €150 now pay a flat €3 customs duty. The duty is charged per *category of goods* (distinct item/tariff line) in the parcel, not per parcel. It applies from 1 Jul 2026 to 1 Jul 2028; one source says to 30 Jun 2028. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/eu-ends-eur150-duty-exemption-charging-shein-and-temu-eur3-per-item/); [E-Commerce Institute Cologne](https://ecommerceinstitut.de/eu-package-fee-from-1-july-2026-what-the-new-customs-rules-mean-for-temu-shein-and-european-e-commerce/); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/eu-taxes-low-value-parcels-aliexpress-shein-temu/); [Tovima](https://www.tovima.com/finance/eu-to-charge-e3-fee-on-shein-temu-parcels-from-july-2026/)
- The flat rate is transitional. Once the **EU Customs Data Hub** is operational in July 2028, it gives way to normal classification-based customs duties, which effectively ends the €150 duty exemption permanently. — [E-Commerce Institute Cologne](https://ecommerceinstitut.de/eu-package-fee-from-1-july-2026-what-the-new-customs-rules-mean-for-temu-shein-and-european-e-commerce/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/eu-ends-eur150-duty-exemption-charging-shein-and-temu-eur3-per-item/)
- Scale: a TechTimes headline (2 Jul 2026) says 5.9 billion imports are now taxed. Only the headline was seen; the body was not verified. — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319496/20260702/eu-customs-duty-hits-shein-temu-parcels-59-billion-imports-now-taxed.htm)
- Platform workaround: before the July deadline, Shein and Temu moved a meaningful share of EU-bound inventory into French, German and Polish 3PLs, so those orders ship as domestic dispatches and avoid the per-parcel duty. — [Novadata](https://novadata.io/resources/news/shein-temu-eu-3-euro-duty-eu-inventory-workaround-july-2026)

**EU €2 handling fee (in force since 1 Nov 2026)**
- The Council of the EU's own policy page says it gave **final approval in September 2026** to the customs reform. The reform creates an EU-wide handling fee on small distance-selling consignments: **€2 per item**, set by the Commission and applied by all member states **from 1 Nov 2026**. — [Council of the EU (Consilium)](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-action-influx-of-small-parcels/)
- Legal basis as cited by secondary sources: Commission Delegated Regulation **(EU) 2026/2152**, reportedly published in the OJ on 22 Oct 2026, and Article 20 of the new Union Customs Code, Regulation **(EU) 2026/2108**. Neither citation was verified against EUR-Lex. — [Dimweigh](https://dimweigh.com/blog/eu-2-euro-handling-fee-november-2026); [Passport](https://passportglobal.com/blog/blog-eu-handling-fee/); [Taxually](https://www.taxually.com/blog/eu-handling-fee-3-euro-customs-duty)
- The fee applies to B2C distance sales of imported goods *regardless of value*, is non-refundable, and stacks on top of the €3 duty. — [Hellotax](https://hellotax.com/blog/eu-3-euro-customs-duty-2-euro-handling-fee/); [Passport](https://passportglobal.com/blog/blog-eu-handling-fee/); [WorldEF, 31 Oct 2026](https://worldef.com/2026/10/31/eu-2-euro-ecommerce-import-handling-fee/)
- Sources conflict on the unit of charge: most say per item; one says per distinct item category in the customs declaration; another says per declaration line. An earlier report described the €3 itself as a "handling fee", and one headline mentions a fee "rising to €5" (unverified). — [Hellotax](https://hellotax.com/blog/eu-3-euro-customs-duty-2-euro-handling-fee/); [AX Holding](https://www.axholdinggroup.com/en/blog/eu-customs-handling-fee); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/eu-taxes-low-value-parcels-aliexpress-shein-temu/)
- Data requirement: one source in the results says the EU requires sellers to provide detailed product reference information from 1 Nov 2026. Its attribution within the results was not confirmed. — [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/eu-parcel-fee-de-minimis-shein-temu-cross-border-dtc); [nShift readiness guide](https://nshift.com/blog/eu-customs-changes-ecommerce-2026)
- Temu's exposure: an older Substack analysis says Europe had become Temu's largest market, at about 40% of global sales, while US growth stalled under tariffs. — [Tech Buzz China, Temu Watch](https://techbuzzchina.substack.com/p/temu-watch-10-logistics-in-times)

**UK £135 Low Value Import relief**
- The UK government will remove the customs-duty relief for consignments of £135 or less. On **13 Jul 2026** HMRC and HM Treasury confirmed the relief will be replaced by new arrangements taking effect **by October 2028 at the latest**, six months earlier than the previously planned March 2029. — [Meridian Global Services](https://meridianglobalservices.com/uk-to-abolish-135-low-value-import-relief-by-october-2028/); [GOV.UK: Reforming customs rules for low value imports](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reforming-customs-rules-for-low-value-imports); earlier March 2029 timing: [Marosa VAT](https://marosavat.com/vat-news/uk-remove-customs-duty-exemption-by-2029); [Deloitte Taxscape (Autumn Budget 2025)](https://taxscape.deloitte.com/measures-autumn-budget-2025/customs-duty-relief-for-low-value-imports.aspx)
- The replacement regime has dedicated arrangements for consignments of £135 or less, with customs duty paid to HMRC **quarterly after goods enter the UK**. A separate relief for private gifts of £39 or less is retained. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reforming-customs-rules-for-low-value-imports); [Menzies](https://www.menzies.co.uk/uk-low-value-import-reform-what-changes-mean/)
- Until the change takes effect, consignments of £135 or less remain free of customs duty. One source claims the relief ends 31 Dec 2026, which conflicts with the official timeline and should be treated as unreliable. — [Dimweigh UK](https://dimweigh.com/blog/uk-135-import-duty-relief-removal); [Global Corporate Logistics](https://globalcorporatelogistics.com/low-value-imports-135-in-2026-what-might-change-and-how-uk-sellers-should-prepare/)
- Comparison of EU parcel fee vs UK relief: [Netz-Trends](https://www.netz-trends.de/uk/eu-parcel-fee-uk-low-value-import-relief/)
- On the export side, UK goods face the 10% tier of the US forced-labor 301, and EU goods are capped at 10% net of MFN. This matters for EU/UK sellers exporting to US customers. — [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/forced-labour-section-301-final-action)

### Inferences
- **EU per-order math:** a three-SKU China-direct order of €30 now carries about €9 of flat duty (3 × €3, if each SKU is a separate tariff category) plus about €6 of handling fees (3 × €2), on top of import VAT. That is roughly €15 in new fixed charges. This structurally favors single-item, higher-value orders and EU-stocked inventory, which is exactly the Shein/Temu response. Exact per-item vs per-category treatment changes the math materially.
- **UK is now the relatively more lenient European market** for China-direct dropshipping through about Oct 2028: no customs duty on consignments of £135 or less, with VAT still collected at point of sale. This window closes by Oct 2028.
- **Dropshipping implication for Europe:** EU-warehouse fulfillment, using CJ's German warehouse or EU 3PLs, avoids both the €3 and €2 charges because the goods are imported in bulk under normal classification. It becomes the default for sub-€20 products, mirroring the US shift.

I did not verify whether the 2026 customs reform changes the €150 IOSS ceiling, makes platforms "deemed importers"/deemed suppliers for customs, or links IOSS to the new fee.

## 6. Product categories hit hardest vs least, and sourcing countries with tariff advantages

### Takeaway
**Hardest hit:**
- Low-priced (under $20 retail) China-sourced impulse goods, which lost de minimis.
- Metal-intensive goods: Section 232 steel/aluminum/copper at 50% on full value and 25% on derivatives.
- Upholstered furniture and cabinets: 25% Section 232.
- Toys and game consoles: not exempt from the 12.5% forced-labor 301, and China-origin items also carry legacy 301.
- Apparel: no broad exemption.

**Least hit:** laptops, smartphones, networking gear and semiconductors (HTS lines in Note 52(b) annexes) are exempt from the forced-labor 301. The exemption is HTS-specific; solar modules are not exempt.

**Country advantage under the forced-labor tiers:**
- Mexico/Canada (USMCA-qualifying goods exempt).
- India, UK, and EU/Taiwan (10% tiers).
- Vietnam/China (12.5%). China carries the extra legacy 301 layer.

### Cited Findings
- **Electronics exemption:** laptops (HTS 8471.30) and smartphones (8517.13) are in the Note 52(b) exemption list. One guide also lists computers (8471), network equipment (8517.62) and semiconductors (8541/8542) as exempt, but not solar modules (8541.43). Another says the annexes cover computers, servers, semiconductors, telecom equipment and batteries. The exemption is line-specific, not "electronics" generally. — [Carra Globe: Section 301 electronics exemption](https://carraglobe.com/section-301-electronics-exemption/); [Zonos docs](https://zonos.com/docs/guides/country-guides/united-states/section-301-forced-labor); [HTS Lookup](https://htslookup.com/programs/section-301/forced-labor/)
- **Toys and game consoles** are not on the exempt list and pay the 12.5% forced-labor 301. — [Zentriaflow checker](https://zentriaflow.com/forced-labor-tariff); [Gingercontrol](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/importing-from-china-tariff-guide)
- **Apparel:** there is no broad exemption except CAFTA-DR textile/apparel and Jordan. Apparel from Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia pays the standard 10% until tariff-rate quotas are implemented. — [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/forced-labor-meet-section-301-new-tariffs-target-60-of-americas-biggest-trading-partners/); [Tarifflens: apparel from China](https://www.tarifflens.ai/blog/section-301-tariffs-apparel-china-2026)
- **Universal forced-labor 301 exemptions:** goods under listed Section 232 headings, energy (Ch. 27), coffee, tea, spices, cocoa beans, natural rubber, gold, beef, most medicines and works of art. — [STR Trade](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/tariff-actions-resources/section-301-investigations-forced-labor); [Zonos docs](https://zonos.com/docs/guides/country-guides/united-states/section-301-forced-labor)
- **Section 232 metals:** 50% for articles wholly or mostly of steel/aluminum/copper; 25% for derivatives; 15% or less metal by value is out of scope. — [KPMG](https://www.kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/04/united-states-new-rules-calculating-section-232-tariffs-steel-aluminum-copper.html)
- **Furniture/cabinets/vanities:** 25% under Section 232, per a secondary source. — [ChinaManufacturers](https://www.chinamanufacturers.com/guides/section-232-tariffs/)
- **Price-point effect** (vendor/blog sources):
  - Products under a $20 selling price are increasingly hard to make profitable after tariffs.
  - Categories cited as safer: fashion accessories, beauty, pet supplies and novelty gifts (often lower List 4A 7.5% exposure), plus home/kitchen accessories, automotive accessories and office/WFH gear.
  - Electronics are cited as the weakest category, though that claim's "25–145%" range reflects superseded 2025 rates.
  - Higher-ticket items ($200+), branded stores and print-on-demand with domestic fulfillment are cited as most resilient.
  - — [OneShipPros](https://oneshippros.com/china-tariffs-2026-how-they-affect-your-dropshipping-business/); [Nventory: best products 2026](https://nventory.io/blog/best-dropshipping-products-2026-what-to-sell); [Grey Journal](https://greyjournal.net/hustle/grow/is-dropshipping-dead-2026/); [TechTraq](https://www.techtraq.com/blog/real-cost-dropshipping-2026)
- **Country tiers:**
  - India, Mexico, Canada and UK: 10%.
  - EU and Taiwan: capped at 10% net of MFN.
  - Japan, Korea and Switzerland: capped at 12.5% net of MFN.
  - China, Vietnam, Brazil and Russia: 12.5%, with China and Brazil also paying legacy 301.
  - USMCA-qualifying goods: exempt.
  - — [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/forced-labour-section-301-final-action); [International Trade Insights](https://www.internationaltradeinsights.com/2026/07/ustr-imposes-new-section-301-tariffs-targeting-forced-labor-concerns-across-60-economies/)
- India's 10% currently undercuts Vietnam's 12.5%. A pending excess-capacity 301 could add about 8% to India, and the US has reportedly promised India a "comparative tariff advantage". — [EcoNiti](https://www.econiti.org/daily-news/2026-09-23/2026-09-23-india-us-trade-deal-section-301/); [MLQ](https://mlq.ai/news/us-assures-india-of-a-tariff-edge-over-asian-rivals-in-their-trade-deal-indian-officials-say/)
- In the EU, duty under the €3 flat regime is the same regardless of origin or product category, until the 2028 data hub. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/eu-ends-eur150-duty-exemption-charging-shein-and-temu-eur3-per-item/)

### Inferences
- **Still viable to dropship into the US:**
  - Products that are higher AOV, lightweight and non-metal.
  - Products on China Section 301 List 4A (7.5%) rather than Lists 1–3 (25%).
  - Electronics HTS lines exempt from the forced-labor 301, such as phones, laptops and some accessories classed under those lines. Classification-dependent.
  - Products stocked in US warehouses.
- **Hardest:**
  - Metal-heavy home, kitchen and tool goods (232).
  - Furniture.
  - Cheap multi-unit apparel, toys and novelties shipped China-direct.
- **Into the EU:** the flat per-item €3 + €2 charge hurts the cheapest items regardless of category. Multi-SKU baskets of trinkets are hit hardest, and single higher-value items are least affected.
- **Sourcing diversification:** Vietnam, India and Mexico avoid China's legacy 25% List 1–3 layer, but most dropship catalogs (AliExpress/CJ) remain China-origin. The tariff difference is now roughly 7.5–25 points rather than the 2025 extremes.

### Gaps
- No authoritative mapping of top dropshipping SKUs to China 301 List 1–3 vs 4A vs exempt. The blog claim that accessories, beauty and pet supplies are "5–7.5%" was not verified.
- Section 232 actions on other consumer-relevant categories in 2026 (e.g., trucks, lumber, robotics, drones, pharma) were not fully catalogued.

## 7. Other geopolitical and supply-chain events (2025–2026): Iran war/Hormuz, Red Sea/Suez, export controls, rare earths, freight rates, and the Nov 2026 midterms

### Takeaway
**2026 conflict.** The dominant 2026 shock was the US-Israel war on Iran, which began with strikes on 28 Feb 2026. The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed for most of the year: a tracker reports 3 transits on 8 Nov 2026 vs about 85 a day normally. Brent topped $100 on 8 Mar 2026, peaking around $126. Fuel, insurance and air-cargo costs rose.

**Freight.** Container lines began returning to Suez/Red Sea routes in September 2026. By mid-November container rates were falling for a third week, but they remain far above a year earlier:
- Drewry WCI composite: $4,305/FEU on 12 Nov.
- Shanghai–LA: $7,416/FEU.
- Shanghai–NY: $9,020/FEU.

**Rare earths.** China's rare-earth control pause runs to 10 Jan 2027.

**Midterms.** Democrats won both chambers in the 3 Nov 2026 midterms. Analysts say this does not by itself change tariffs, because presidential authority under Sections 301/232 stays in place and veto overrides need two-thirds.

### Inferences
- **Freight costs for dropshippers.** Ocean freight now matters more than it did under the parcel model, because dropshipping has shifted to bulk-import-then-domestic-fulfill. With transpacific at about $7.4–9.0k/FEU, roughly double year-ago, warehoused supply carries higher per-unit freight and inventory-financing costs, which are passed through by CJ and similar suppliers. Q1 2027 Lunar New Year and carrier GRIs could lift rates again.
- **Policy outlook.** Policy volatility is likely to persist into 2027:
  - The 9–10 Jan 2027 US-China deadlines.
  - The excess-capacity 301 outcome.
  - The Section 122 Federal Circuit appeal.
  - Congressional oversight from a Democratic Congress, mainly through hearings, Section 122/301 reform bills and appropriations riders rather than repeal.
  - The de minimis end is bipartisan and statutory (1 Jul 2027), so it is unlikely to be reversed.
- **Europe-bound freight.** Red Sea/Suez normalization lowers Asia–Europe freight and transit times for EU-warehoused stock, partly offsetting the new EU parcel fees for sellers who move to bulk import.
