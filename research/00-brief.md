# Research brief (refined from the original ask)

## Original ask (paraphrased)
"Not another generic B2B SaaS / AI-workflow tool. The last few years introduced a lot of new
technology. There must be new services in demand that nobody is building. Do deep research on
all new technology, popular and low-key, especially advances that are *implied* (second-order)
and that I wouldn't think of. Identify market gaps and feasibility to build. Think outside the
box. Include Western Europe. Research autonomously until done."

## Refined objective
Produce a ranked shortlist of 15–25 concrete, buildable opportunities that exist *because* of a
technology, regulatory, or behavioural shift from 2023–2026, where:
1. The opportunity is a *service or product for a real buyer*, not "AI agent for X workflow".
2. The enabling shift is recent enough that incumbents haven't closed the gap.
3. A small technical team (1–3 people, < €100k, 6–9 months) can ship a credible v1.
4. Western Europe (DE, FR, NL, BE, UK, Nordics, ES, IT, CH, AT) is a plausible first market,
   ideally with an EU-specific advantage (regulation, language fragmentation, labour shortage,
   grid/energy structure, sovereignty demand) that US startups won't chase first.

## Assumptions (state and revisit)
- Founder profile: solo or tiny team, strong software skills, can learn hardware integration,
  limited capital, based in or targeting Western Europe.
- "Generic" = horizontal AI copilots, workflow automation, CRM/PM tooling, prompt tools,
  AI SDR/agent marketplaces, chat-with-your-docs.

## Method
Eight parallel research tracks. Each track answers the same five questions per finding:
  (a) What changed, when, evidence with URLs (2024–2026 sources preferred).
  (b) What does it *imply* that isn't obvious? (second-order: what becomes cheap, mandatory,
      possible, or broken)
  (c) Who is now in pain or newly able to pay? Size the wedge roughly.
  (d) Who is already building it? Where exactly is the gap?
  (e) Feasibility for the founder profile: build cost, moat, regulatory risk, distribution.

Tracks:
 T1 Frontier AI capability shifts and their second-order consequences (not AI tools themselves)
 T2 EU regulation-created markets 2025–2028 (mandates with dates)
 T3 Energy, grid, batteries, heat, EV, home electrification in Europe
 T4 Hardware/physical: robotics, drones, sensors, AR/wearables, BCI, LiDAR, 3D printing, space
 T5 Bio, health, longevity, GLP-1, diagnostics, digital health regulation in EU
 T6 Money, identity, payments, crypto/stablecoins, EU digital identity wallet, e-invoicing
 T7 Software substrate shifts: protocols, local-first, WebGPU, agentic commerce, on-device, IoT
 T8 Behavioural, demographic, cultural and geopolitical shifts in Western Europe

## Output
- One synthesis report: shortlist ranked by (demand evidence × timing × feasibility × EU edge),
  each with a one-paragraph thesis, the "why now", the buyer, the v1, the moat, the risks,
  and what to validate in the first 2 weeks.
- A "rejected / crowded" list so the reader knows what was considered.
- An appendix per track with raw findings and sources.
