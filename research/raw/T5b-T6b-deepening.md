# Part B — Ground the two reports never reached

## B1. The EU Data Act — the biggest thing both reports missed

Regulation **(EU) 2023/2854** has applied since **12 September 2025**, and the **access-by-design obligation (Art. 3(1)) applies to connected products placed on the market from 12 September 2026** — i.e. now. Users of connected products (explicitly including **wearables, smartwatches, fitness trackers and medical devices**) have a right to the **raw and pre-processed data** their device generates, free of charge, in a **structured, commonly used, machine-readable format**, with real-time access where feasible, requestable by simple electronic means. Under **Article 5** a user can instruct the data holder to make that data available to a **third party of the user's choosing**, on **FRAND** terms; the holder cannot charge the user but may agree reasonable, non-discriminatory compensation with the recipient. The Commission closed a draft-guidelines consultation on 20 February 2026. Enforcement is national and nascent — only **the Netherlands and Malta** had complete setups, with DE, SE, FI, AT, DK and BE still appointing authorities. **Garmin already publishes an EU Data Act information page.** [[Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/data-act-explained), [Eraneos on Art. 5](https://www.eraneos.com/articles/eu-data-act-article-5-and-third-party-data-sharing-on-frand-terms-how-to-comply-and-compete-now/), [gamingtechlaw.com](https://www.gamingtechlaw.com/2026/08/data-act-access-by-design/), [Garmin](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/legal/eu-data-act/handheld-devices/)] **[S]**

Second-order reading **[I]**: the wearable-API negotiation of A10 is being replaced by a statutory right. A European app need not qualify for Garmin's Health API programme — it can be **designated by the user** as an Art. 5 recipient. Nobody is yet packaging that right for consumers. Adjacent regime: matchmaking between data holders and users can make you a **data intermediation service** under the Data Governance Act, requiring registration (ACM in NL, Arcep in FR) — an option, probably not an obligation, for a single-purpose consumer app. [[ACM](https://www.acm.nl/en/publications/data-governance-act-registration-data-intermediation-service-providers-and-application-eu-label-now-online)]

# Top 5 revised opportunities across health and money

**1. A Data Act "data agent" for wearables and connected health devices (EU-wide, DE/NL first).** Since 12 September 2025 every user of a connected product has a statutory right to their device's raw and pre-processed data in machine-readable form, and under Article 5 can direct the holder to hand it to a third party on FRAND terms; from 12 September 2026 new products must be built so access works by default. That converts wearable integration from a commercial negotiation (Garmin's reported $5,000 fee, WHOOP/Oura membership gates, Polar's 90-day cap) into an entitlement the *user* exercises. Build the thing that helps a European consumer exercise it across their devices and makes the result useful: a longitudinal strength, body-composition and activity record that survives switching from Garmin to Oura to WHOOP, with lab PDFs alongside and a one-page GP-ready export. This is the founder's existing PWA plus a rights-exercise workflow. Non-diagnostic storage and display stays outside MDR; AI Act Art. 50 applies from 2 August 2026. **Risk:** enforcement is embryonic (only NL and Malta complete), so holders can stall — start with vendors already publishing Data Act pages. **[S] law, [I] product.**

**2. The German PKV "documented therapy concept" evidence pack.** On 21 May 2026 the Landgericht Nürnberg-Fürth (8 O 4860/25) held that a private insurer need not reimburse Mounjaro where the insured skipped cheaper basic measures and produced no documented structured therapy concept — while leaving the door open where the drug sits inside a documented, physician-monitored multimodal concept with comorbidities. Germany has **8.79m PKV-vollversicherte**, **>4m households** on or considering GLP-1, GKV exclusion under §34 SGB V, an oral option on sale since 1 September 2026, and an established evidentiary standard (the MMK: ≥6 months of documented nutrition **and** exercise therapy with certified professionals, evidenced by a Bescheinigung). Build the file: a structured six-month log with professional sign-off, adherence evidence, comorbidity documentation and an insurer-ready PDF, at €15–25/month to people about to claim €2,400–4,800/year. Then ladder the same programme into a **ZPP-certified §20 course** for the post-loss cohort (below BMI 30, so eligible), unlocking both consumer reimbursement and the **§3 Nr. 34 EStG €600/year employer budget** — via the live-stream IKT route to avoid the ISO 27001 gate. **Risk:** one first-instance judgment; and you must not give legal advice.

**3. First DiPA through the new Erprobungszulassung (Germany).** The DiPA register has been empty since 2022 because the old evidence bar had to be cleared before filing. The First Ordinance amending the DiPAV came into force **1 July 2026**, the application portal went live the same day, BfArM published a revised guideline **15 July 2026**, and provisional listing is now available for **up to one year** on a *plausible* expected care benefit plus a scientific evaluation concept from an independent institution. Reimbursement is **€40/month** plus up to **€30/month** for accompanying support, and **a DiPA need not be a medical device**. Germany has 5.7m Pflegebedürftige heading for 6.2m by 2030. The natural product, adjacent to this founder's competence and unserved by the box-and-alarm incumbents: **strength, balance and fall-prevention for people with Pflegegrad 1–2 at home, plus a back-and-lifting programme for their informal carers**, with the evaluation concept co-authored by a university sports-science or nursing department. Being first in an empty register is worth more than the €40. **Risk:** the evaluation partner is a real dependency; the 2027 Pflegereform could move the amounts.

**4. Inspection-ready wraparound evidence for the UK *private* GLP-1 channel.** The NHS half of T5's thesis is closed — NHS England centrally commissioned BSOP from 23 June 2025 with Oviva and Xyla delivering. The private half is bigger and unserved: roughly **2 million UK adults** pay out of pocket, ~7× the NHS number; Voy alone has 100,000+ members; and on 16 April 2026 the GPhC published a report naming exactly what inspectors found missing — risk assessment, consultation documentation, third-party assurance, **independent BMI verification**, clinical follow-up — across 1,307 concerns, 49% about online pharmacies. Sell GPhC-registered providers a white-labelled patient-facing programme whose by-product is the audit trail: verified weight and waist capture, a nausea-aware two-sessions-a-week strength protocol with a protein floor, functional proxies (chair-stand, grip), side-effect logging and escalation flags, exported as a per-patient support record, priced per active patient per month. The ENDO 2026 data is what makes generic "move more" content indefensible. **Risk:** stay out of dose decisions; regulated buyers procure slowly.

**5. "AML in a box" for one newly obliged sector before 10 July 2027.** AMLR (EU) 2024/1624 applies from **10 July 2027**, bringing traders in high-value goods above **€10,000** (luxury vehicles, yachts, aircraft, jewellery, art), crowdfunding providers and intermediaries into scope, with football clubs and agents from 2029 and an EU-wide €10,000 cash cap. Tens of thousands of firms with 1–20 staff will need a risk assessment, CDD with register-native KYB and UBO checks, PEP/sanctions screening, record-keeping, FIU templates and a training log — today a Word template. Pick **one sector in one or two countries** and ship the full per-language workflow at €50–200/month. The build is easier than T6 assumed because the register layer is commoditised (OpenRegister, Topograph for DE; INPI-bridging intermediaries for FR) and **AMLD6 requires legitimate-interest, machine-readable UBO access from July 2026**. The 2026–27 sales window matches a 6–9-month build. **Risk:** per-country content depth is both the moat and the cost.

**Runners-up.** (a) **NL GLI as a funded distribution channel** (B3) — partner with an RIVM-recognised programme owner rather than seek level-II recognition alone. (b) **GLP-1 member retention sold to European strength gyms** (B5) — no European chain programme exists. (c) **Flat-fee age/attribute gate for SME shops** (A13) — demand strengthened sharply, but RP registration only applies from 24 December 2026; build against Italy's IT-Wallet with a Yoti/VerifyMy fallback.
